# The 4D framework

From Anthropic's AI Fluency work. **AI fluency** is the ability to work with AI systems
effectively, efficiently, ethically and safely.

The four Ds are competencies, not steps. They interleave in practice, and a real dialogue moves
between them rather than marching through in order.

---

## Delegation — deciding what to hand over

Three parts:

- **Problem awareness** — what am I actually trying to accomplish? What does done look like?
  This is the human's alone. An AI cannot know what outcome they are really after.
- **Platform awareness** — what is this system genuinely good and bad at, for *this* task?
  This is where you contribute most. Most people underestimate the range and overestimate the
  reliability.
- **Task delegation** — drawing the line. Which parts go to the AI, which stay human, and in what
  order. The line moves with stakes: high stakes pull work back to the human, or add a checkpoint.

The common failure at both extremes: handing over the judgment call that should have stayed
human, or hand-holding work that could have been handed over whole.

## Description — communicating what you want

Three levels, all three usually needed:

- **Product description** — what you want as an outcome. Format, length, audience, constraints,
  what done looks like. Most under-specified in practice.
- **Process description** — how it should get there. Steps to follow, what to do first, what to
  avoid, whether to check in. Prescribe process when the route matters; leave it open when it
  does not.
- **Performance description** — how the AI should behave. The role it plays: skeptic, pair,
  executor, tutor, editor. Tone, how much pushback, whether to ask before acting.

Vague in, vague out. Most disappointing results trace back to a missing product or performance
description rather than to the model.

## Discernment — evaluating critically

Mirrors description, three levels:

- **Product discernment** — is the output actually good? Accurate, complete, fit for purpose.
- **Process discernment** — was the approach sound? Good answers reached by bad reasoning will
  not survive contact with the next case.
- **Performance discernment** — is it behaving as a good collaborator? Over-agreeing, drifting
  from the role, hiding uncertainty.

The practical move is to decide what "good" means **before** the work, not after. Judging output
you already have is much harder than checking it against criteria you set in advance.

## Diligence — responsible use

Three parts:

- **Creation diligence** — should this be made at all? Is the approach honest?
- **Transparency diligence** — who needs to know AI was involved, and how much?
- **Deployment diligence** — you own what you ship, whoever wrote it. Verification before it goes
  anywhere that matters.

Diligence sets the depth of the other three. Low stakes means a light pass; high stakes means a
tighter delegation line, a fuller description, and a real verification step.

---

## The three interaction modes

Related, and useful vocabulary for the Delegation conversation:

- **Automation** — the AI executes a defined task; the human specifies and checks.
- **Augmentation** — human and AI work together, thinking alongside each other.
- **Agency** — the human configures an AI system to act with some independence.

Most tasks are augmentation even when people describe them as automation. Naming the mode out
loud often resolves a delegation question by itself.
